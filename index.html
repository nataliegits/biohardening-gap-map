What we still don't know about disinfecting indoor air

Far-UVC and glycol vapor both work on something. Anyone deciding whether to install them needs to know which organism, at what dose, in which rooms, and at what cost to the people breathing in it. This map codes what I found by target, setting, model system and endpoint. Darker cells have more evidence behind them. A pale cell with a dash means I found nothing.

Glycol vapor

propylene · dipropylene · triethylene

The efficacy evidence splits into two eras. The 1940s work covers wards and barracks with methods that predate modern aerobiology. The modern work is mostly one product and one phage. On the safety side the toxicology is animal inhalation, and the only human-cell data comes from vaping research.

Reading list

Every study coded above, grouped by matrix and ordered roughly as they should be read: the anchor papers first, then the ones that qualify them. Each title links to the DOI or the repository copy. Most are open access; for the 1940s papers the link goes to the free PubMed Central scan.

How to read an empty cell. An empty cell means my search found nothing. It does not mean no study exists. The scan covered indexed literature reachable by search in November 2026 and is weighted toward English-language work and toward papers that name their organism and setting in the abstract. Treat every empty cell as a lead to check.

Shading is my own judgement of how settled a cell is, not a count of papers. Level 4 means several independent groups with consistent results. Level 1 means one study, an indirect measurement, or evidence borrowed from a different exposure regime. Where a cell carries a caveat flag, the flag is doing more work than the color.

Scope. Efficacy columns escalate in realism, so a filled cell on the left says nothing about the one on its right. Far-UVC safety is split by tissue because the skin argument and the eye argument rest on different penetration depths and have very different amounts of human data behind them.
